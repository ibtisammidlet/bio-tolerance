I'm aware of the controversies over sensitisation/tolerance, the warring theories where some people say it's just a way of describing a magnification of negative results, etc. but I think people who jump to conclusiojns like that ought to consider the fact that sensitisation, disparate from tolerance/tachyphylaxis in the rat models, is SPECIFIC TO SINGLE DOSE. Repeated dosing causes reduction in effect. Dosing in intervals cause the sensitisation effect. This is clearly something different than just one single process of tolerance. If sensitisation was Just Bad or Not a Thing then according to these studies, you should take amphetamine repeatedly and that would be better for you. Obviously that's not the case. Secondly, the literature shows that sensitisation is dependent on environmental richness/novelty. Without rat wheels and rat forests, the rats don't sensitize. I link this to the fact that a change of scenery has been established as causing an actual physical diminishment of opioid tolerance, to the extent that it's known that heroin addicts can use the same dose in a totally different environment, which in the old environment is tolerated but in the new environment kills them. That is real. Sensitisation is sensitisation. It's just that a real understanding of what tolerance/sensitization processes entail would actually be more specified than two words or directions. Sometimes good effects can sensitize. Sometimes they can tolerate. Sometimes bad effects can sensitize. Sometimes they can tolerate -- that's fortunate, otherwise I would still have the appetite from day 1 and would then be the slender man.
