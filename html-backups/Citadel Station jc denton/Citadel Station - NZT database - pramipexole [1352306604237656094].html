Proposed Mechanisms for NMDA Involvement in DAWS

The pathophysiological mechanism connecting DAWS and NMDA overactivation likely involves: 1. During pramipexole treatment: Pramipexole's D2/D3 receptor agonism suppresses NMDA receptor activity and glutamate signaling.[7] 2. Adaptive changes: Chronic treatment leads to compensatory upregulation of NMDA receptors and altered glutamatergic transmission, similar to mechanisms observed in alcohol dependence by Freund and Anderson (1996).[8] 3. Upon withdrawal: Cessation of dopaminergic inhibition unmasks the upregulated glutamatergic system. As Krystal et al. (1998) demonstrated in related contexts, this can result in glutamatergic hyperactivity.[9] 4. Clinical manifestation: The excessive NMDA receptor activity contributes to the constellation of withdrawal symptoms characteristic of DAWS. This mechanism shares similarities with other withdrawal syndromes. For example, Tsai et al. (1998) documented that ethanol withdrawal symptoms are partially mediated by NMDA receptor upregulation.[10]
