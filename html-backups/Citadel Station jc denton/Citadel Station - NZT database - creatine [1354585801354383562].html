Single-dose oral guanidinoacetic acid exhibits dose-dependent pharmacokinetics in healthy volunteers

Study Overview and Background

First study to describe the pharmacokinetic outcomes after ingestion of guanidinoacetic acid (GAA). Published in Nutrition Research in 2015 by Sergej M. Ostojic and Aleksandra Vojvodic-Ostojic from institutions in Serbia. GAA is the natural precursor of creatine in the body, synthesized endogenously from L-arginine and glycine. GAA has potential as a dietary supplement due to being more bioavailable, stable, and cost-effective than creatine. GAA is more soluble in water than creatine (3600 mg/L vs 1330 mg/L). Supplemental GAA may be "semiessential" when endogenous GAA synthesis is limited. GAA powder costs approximately 40% less than creatine.

Study Design and Methods

Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, parallel-group trial with 48 participants. 24 men and 24 women, mean age 22±2 years, weight 71±14 kg, height 176±10 cm. Participants randomly assigned to receive single oral doses of GAA (1.2g, 2.4g, 4.8g) or placebo (inulin). ⏱️ Blood samples collected before (0 hours) and at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24 hours post-ingestion. Plasma GAA and creatine assessed using high-performance liquid chromatography. One-compartment model used for pharmacokinetic analysis. ️ Standardized meals provided with no creatine-rich foods (meat, fish, milk).

Key Pharmacokinetic Findings for GAA

Lag Time and Absorption

⏰ Similar lag time across all doses: 0.14±0.17h (low), 0.31±0.18h (medium), 0.38±0.32h (high); p=0.05. Time to peak concentration: ~90 minutes for low and medium doses, 2.2 hours for high dose. ‍♂️ Mean absorption half-time: <40 minutes for low/medium doses, ~60 minutes for high dose.

Peak Concentration and Exposure

Peak plasma concentration (μmol/L): 56.9±9.2 (low), 151.3±17.5 (medium), 418.4±89.6 (high). Area under curve (AUC) increased in greater than dose-proportional manner. AUC (μmol/L*h): 224.8±38.0 (low), 531.4±64.1 (medium), 2092.4±453.2 (high); p<0.0001. Strong relationship between AUC and dose: AUC = 102.63 * e^(0.652 * Dose of GAA [g]).

Distribution and Elimination

Volume of distribution (L): 102.6±17.3 (low), 97.5±15.7 (medium), 61.1±12.7 (high); p<0.0001. ⏱️ Elimination half-time: similar for low/medium doses (<1.75h), longer for high dose (>2.1h); p=0.001. Apparent systemic clearance (L/h): 46.9±8.3 (low), 39.1±4.6 (medium), 20.5±4.4 (high); p<0.0001.

Creatine Response to GAA

Ingestion of GAA elevated plasma creatine compared to placebo: +80% (1.2g), +116% (2.4g), +293% (4.8g); p<0.0001. ⏱️ Peak creatine concentration occurred within ~2 hours for all doses. Peak creatine concentration (μmol/L): 51.7±7.8 (low), 67.9±17.1 (medium), 113.3±31.3 (high). Mean terminal half-life values for creatine: between 5.8-8.8 hours. Less than proportional increase in AUC for plasma creatine with increasing GAA doses.

Mechanisms and Explanations

GAA is subject to saturable metabolism in the dose range tested. GAA transporter has Km value of ~80 μmol; high-dose GAA (plasma levels up to 418 μmol/L) might saturate transport. Reduced clearance in high-dose GAA likely due to saturation of hepatic metabolism. GAA N-methyltransferase (GAMT) that converts GAA to creatine might become saturated with higher doses. Higher-dose GAA might bind more extensively to blood proteins, explaining volume of distribution differences. Better tissue delivery potentially occurs with low-dose GAA (higher volume estimate indicates more extensive distribution).
9:38 PM

️ Safety Considerations

✅ No participants reported adverse effects from GAA supplementation. Baseline plasma GAA (2.9±0.3 μmol/L) well below pathological levels seen in creatine deficiency syndromes. ️ Previous studies show GAA can increase homocysteine formation, though effects appear clinically insignificant. Blood-brain barrier has limited permeability for GAA, making brain accumulation unlikely. Concerns exist that intrastriatal GAA might increase seizure susceptibility and be neurotoxic. More safety studies needed before GAA can be recommended as a dietary supplement.

Study Limitations

No evaluation of pharmacologic/physiological effects of dietary GAA. Study assumed 100% bioavailability after oral administration (based only on animal studies). No urinary kinetics for GAA, creatine, and creatinine provided. Sex differences not analyzed due to small sample sizes. Muscle creatine content not monitored. No comparison with equivalent dosages of oral creatine as control.

Conclusions

GAA single-dose PK metrics were nonlinear with respect to dose size. Systemic exposure to GAA increased in greater than dose-proportional manner across 1.2-4.8g range. Absorption was slower for high-dose GAA, suggesting possible saturable intestinal transport. GAA elimination is dose-dependent, decreasing as dose increases. ️‍♂️ Dietary GAA affected plasma creatine in less than dose-proportional manner. All tested GAA doses (1.2g, 2.4g, 4.8g) were well tolerated by participants.

The volume of distribution (Vd) is a pharmacokinetic parameter that relates the total amount of drug in the body to the concentration in the plasma. What the researchers observed was that the low-dose GAA group had a significantly larger volume of distribution (102.6±17.3 L) compared to the high-dose group (61.1±12.7 L). When a substance has a higher volume of distribution, it suggests that more of the drug is distributed into tissues rather than remaining in the bloodstream. The researchers explain this relationship on page 5 of the paper:
"If GAA is more extensively distributed into the tissues, the blood concentration is lower and the volume estimate is higher. This approach indicates that tissue delivery is potentially better when low-dose GAA is administered, compared with the other dosage protocols."

Guanidinoacetic Acid vs. Creatine for Improved Brain and Muscle Creatine Levels: A Superiority Pilot Trial in Healthy Men

Study Overview and Design

Randomized, double-blind, cross-over pilot trial comparing guanidinoacetic acid (GAA) vs. creatine supplementation. ‍ Conducted by Sergej M. Ostojic and colleagues from the University of Novi Sad, Serbia. ‍⚕️ Five healthy young men (age 24.3 ± 3.1 years; BMI 24.9 ± 1.9 kg/m²) participated in the study. ️ Participants received either GAA (3 grams/day) or equimolar creatine (3.4 grams/day) for 4 weeks. After a 4-week washout period, participants crossed over to the other supplement. Clinical assessments were performed after overnight fasting and no exhaustive exercise in previous 24 hours. MR spectroscopy was used to measure tissue creatine in specific brain regions and vastus medialis muscle. Blood samples were analyzed for serum creatine and total homocysteine (T-Hcy).

Baseline Values and Key Findings

Change in Tissue Creatine After 4 Weeks

Vastus medialis muscle: GAA increased by 8.4% vs. creatine by 3.2% (P = 0.04) Frontal white matter: GAA increased by 8.7% vs. creatine by 2.6% (P = 0.07, trend) Middle cerebellar peduncle: GAA increased by 16.2% vs. creatine by 6.1% (P < 0.01) Paracentral gray matter: GAA increased by 10.5% vs. creatine by 3.9% (P = 0.01) Overall, GAA supplementation resulted in approximately 3.3-fold higher tissue creatine levels compared to creatine.

Blood Parameters

Baseline serum creatine: 42.0 ± 3.0 µmol/L After GAA: increased by 19.2 µmol/L (95% CI; 8.9 - 29.5) After creatine: increased by 14.9 µmol/L (95% CI; 2.8 - 27.0) Baseline T-Hcy: 9.9 ± 2.0 µmol/L After GAA: increased by 3.2 µmol/L (95% CI; 1.7 - 8.1) After creatine: increased by 1.6 µmol/L (95% CI; 1.3 - 4.5) ️ No significant differences between interventions in T-Hcy response (P = 0.43). T-Hcy levels remained below clinically relevant threshold (< 15.0 µmol/L) after both interventions.

Proposed Mechanisms

Why GAA May Be More Effective Than Creatine

GAA may have preferable uptake by target tissues via multiple transport mechanisms. Creatine is mainly transported via SLC6A8 (which can also transport GAA). GAA may utilize additional transport channels:
  • SLC6A6
  • GAT2 (gamma-aminobutyric acid transporter 2)
  • Passive diffusion (particularly in the brain)
Once inside tissues, GAA is readily methylated to creatine by guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT). GAA transport systems may be less easily saturated compared to creatine transporters. ✨ Complete or near-complete tissue methylation of GAA to creatine may occur after transport.

Methylation and Homocysteine Production

GAA is methylated to creatine in a reaction requiring S-adenosyl methionine as the methyl donor. ️ This reaction produces S-adenosyl homocysteine, which is hydrolyzed to homocysteine. Previous studies show dietary GAA can drive homocysteine production in a dose-dependent manner. ️ Despite elevations, homocysteine levels remained below clinically concerning thresholds in this study.

️ Safety Considerations and Concerns

Elevated homocysteine is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease, renal dysfunction, and osteoporotic fractures. GAA loading should be approached cautiously due to its effect on increasing homocysteine levels. Concerns exist about potential GAA neurotoxicity based on observations in GAMT deficiency. In GAMT deficiency, GAA accumulates to levels ~300-fold above normal with concurrent creatine deficit. However, dietary GAA accumulating to toxic levels in normal brain is unlikely due to transport limitations. Long-term safety studies are needed before GAA can be recommended as a dietary supplement.
11:46 PM

Significance and Implications

GAA may have applications in neurodegenerative diseases where creatine intervention has shown limited efficacy. The superior effect of GAA on brain creatine levels could open new therapeutic approaches for brain disorders. ️ The enhanced creatine-boosting effect may have implications for both clinical applications and sports nutrition. This was the first human study to directly compare GAA and creatine for enhancing tissue creatine levels. Previous animal studies (McBreairty et al. 2015) showed similar findings in Yucatan pigs.

Study Limitations

Small sample size (n=5) limits generalizability of findings. ⏱️ Short duration (4 weeks) may not capture long-term effects and safety profile. Limited to healthy young men; results may not apply to women, older adults, or clinical populations. No functional outcomes measured (e.g., cognitive performance, muscle strength). Tissue GAA levels were not assessed, only tissue creatine. No placebo arm was included to control for time effects.

Guanidinoacetic Acid with Creatine Compared with Creatine Alone for Tissue Creatine Content, Hyperhomocysteinemia and Exercise Performance

Study Overview & Design

Randomized, double-blind, crossover superiority trial conducted at University of Novi Sad (Dec 2016-Nov 2017) ‍ 14 healthy young men participated (age 24.4 ± 4.0 years, weight 79.9 ± 13.9 kg, height 176.5 ± 7.3 cm) Participants received either:
  • GAA-creatine mixture (1g GAA + 3g creatine daily) or
  • Equimolar creatine alone (4g daily)
⏱️ Each intervention lasted 4 weeks with a 28-day washout period between phases Study's hypothesis: GAA-creatine mixture would result in more powerful rise in brain and skeletal muscle creatine vs. creatine alone

Background & Context

Targeting energy-demanding tissues remains challenging in human nutrition and biomedicine Impaired bioenergetics accompanies various conditions (cardiometabolic diseases, neurodegenerative disorders, high-intensity exercise) Creatine is recognized as a beneficial, safe energy-boosting agent in athletics and clinical settings ️ Creatine's effectiveness can be limited due to transportability and performance constraints GAA (metabolic precursor of creatine) appears as a novel energy-enhancing supplement ️ Previous studies showed GAA may be superior to creatine in facilitating creatine concentrations in human tissues However, GAA loading has potential side effects (hyperhomocysteinemia, possible neurotoxicity) This study is the first human investigation of combined GAA-creatine supplementation

Key Methods & Measurements

Assessment Methods

Proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H-MRS) measured tissue creatine in:
  • Left centrum semiovale white matter
  • Midline occipital gray matter
  • Right vastus medialis muscle Blood tests measured serum GAA, creatine, creatinine, and total homocysteine (tHcy) ️ Physical measurements included:
  • Body weight
  • Isometric forearm strength (handgrip dynamometer)
  • Muscular strength (1-RM bench press and front squat)
  • Vertical jump performance (height, peak/mean anaerobic power)
  • Cardiorespiratory endurance (maximal endurance running test) Adverse effects monitored through open-ended questionnaire

Key Results & Findings

Tissue Creatine Content

Skeletal muscle creatine increased significantly more with GAA-creatine mixture (16.9 ± 20.2% vs. 2.0 ± 6.0%; P = 0.02) Gray matter creatine increased significantly more with GAA-creatine mixture (5.8 ± 5.3% vs. 1.5 ± 3.2%; P = 0.02) White matter showed trend toward greater increase with GAA-creatine (13.9 ± 12.8% vs. 7.3 ± 7.4%; P = 0.18)

Exercise Performance

️ Upper body strength (bench press) improved more with GAA-creatine (6.0% vs. 5.1%; P < 0.01) Lower body strength (front squat) improved less with GAA-creatine vs. creatine alone (P < 0.01) No significant between-group differences in handgrip strength No significant differences in anaerobic or aerobic performance parameters

⚕️ Safety Parameters

️ Less weight gain with GAA-creatine mixture (0.7 ± 0.2 kg vs. 1.6 ± 0.2 kg with creatine alone; P < 0.01) No inter-group differences in serum GAA, creatine, and tHcy Creatine was superior to GAA-creatine blend in elevating serum creatinine (P = 0.01) ❌ No participants experienced hyperhomocysteinemia (tHcy ≥ 15.0 µmol/L) ❌ No elevated serum creatinine above reference range (> 110 µmol/L) Tissue levels of choline remained unaffected by either intervention ️ No subjective side effects reported during either intervention

️ Proposed Mechanisms

Transport Mechanisms

Creatine primarily transported via creatine transporter (CT1) GAA can be transported via multiple pathways:
  • CT1 (creatine transporter)
  • GAT1 (gamma-amino butyric acid transporter)
  • TauT (taurine transporter)
  • Passive diffusion ️ Absorbed GAA becomes methylated to creatine in target cells

Tissue-Specific Effects

GAA-creatine mixture appeared more effective for improving strength in muscle groups with lower baseline strength Authors hypothesize GAA might be favorably absorbed by specific muscle groups with lower initial GAA/creatine levels Addition of GAA likely helps reach target tissues via different membrane transport proteins

Conclusions & Implications

GAA-creatine mixture (1:3) was superior to pure creatine for improving brain and muscle creatine levels The mixture showed better results for upper body muscular strength and less weight gain ⚕️ The formulation (at tested dosages) appeared safe, with no observed hyperhomocysteinemia or brain choline depletion ️ GAA-creatine mixture could be an effective alternative to creatine alone in weight-sensitive situations ️ Potentially beneficial for populations focused on improving specific muscle groups with lower initial strength May have applications for clinical populations with muscle weakness or elderly individuals

Studies with larger, more diverse populations (varying by age, gender, activity level, clinical conditions) ⏱️ Longer duration studies (>4 weeks) controlling for dietary creatine intake More extensive safety profiling Advanced tissue profiling via 31P-MRS to measure phosphocreatine levels Testing other GAA-creatine ratios beyond 1:3 Direct comparison with GAA intervention alone Studies including muscle biopsy to correlate imaging with tissue metabolites Evaluation of GAA as follow-up strategy after traditional creatine loading protocols

Dodecyl Creatine Ester

Dodecyl creatine ester (DCE) represents a significant advancement in creatine supplementation technology. As a modified form of creatine bound to a 12-carbon fatty acid chain, DCE offers unique properties that allow it to overcome limitations of traditional creatine supplements. Initially developed as a therapeutic option for creatine transporter deficiency (CTD), DCE demonstrates promising results in delivering creatine directly to brain cells and bypassing the blood-brain barrier through intranasal administration.

What is it?

A creatine prodrug created by binding creatine to a 12-carbon (dodecyl) fatty acid chain, resulting in an amphiphilic molecule that can penetrate cell membranes without requiring the creatine transporter.[1] Synthesized through an activation process of creatinine's carbonyl moiety by double protection of the cyclic guanidine, followed by ring opening in the presence of dodecanol, and subsequent carbamate deprotection.[2] Designed to bypass the blood-brain barrier and enter brain cells directly, where it is then metabolized by esterases into creatine, making it particularly valuable for conditions where creatine transport is impaired.[3] Primarily developed for treating Creatine Transporter Deficiency (CTD), an X-linked genetic disorder characterized by mutations in the SLC6A8 gene that prevent creatine uptake into the brain.[4] Functions as a lipophilic prodrug that can reach brain parenchyma and neuronal cells without relying on the creatine transporter, allowing for effective creatine delivery in cases where normal transport mechanisms fail.[5]

Cognitive Benefits

Improves cognitive function and restores abundance of key molecular players in brain regions affected by creatine deficiency, resulting in enhanced overall mental performance.[6] Significantly enhances object recognition capabilities in mouse models of creatine transporter deficiency, demonstrating potential for addressing memory and recognition deficits.[4] Increases spontaneous alternation rate in Y-maze tests, indicating improvements in working and spatial memory functions critical for daily cognitive tasks.[6] Rescues the abundance of key proteins like KIF1A and PLCB1 that are correlated with both object recognition and Y-maze test performance, suggesting comprehensive effects on cognitive circuitry.[3]

Mechanisms

️ Bypasses the non-functional creatine transporter at the blood-brain barrier through its lipophilic properties, delivering creatine directly to neurons and glial cells.[5] Enters brain cells independently of the creatine transporter, where esterases convert it to free creatine, thereby increasing intracellular creatine levels.[2] Modulates expression of neuronal markers related to cognitive function, including proteins involved in neuronal signaling and synaptic plasticity.[7]

Effects on Neurotransmitters/Hormones/Receptors/Pathways

Regulates the NF-κβ pathway by rescuing IκBα protein levels in creatine transporter deficient mouse models, influencing neuroinflammation, memory formation, and synaptic plasticity.[6] Alters pro-BDNF/BDNF ratio, influencing long-term potentiation (LTP) biomarkers linked to cognitive function improvement and neuronal health.[6] Modifies abundance of presynaptic protein IgSF8, a critical regulator of brain microcircuits and neuronal function, affecting synaptic transmission efficiency.[3] (edited)

Neurological Benefits

Treats X-linked intellectual disability caused by creatine transporter deficiency by delivering creatine directly to brain cells, bypassing the transport limitations.[8] ️ Mitigates cognitive decline associated with creatine deficiency in the brain, potentially preserving neurological function and preventing development of severe symptoms.[4] Improves expression of neuronal markers involved in brain development and function, addressing underlying biochemical deficiencies in neurodevelopmental disorders.[7] Helps replenish creatine levels in cerebral cells, particularly in neurons, supporting energy metabolism essential for proper brain function.[8] Shows promise in addressing autism-like symptoms, expressive speech delays, and movement disorders associated with creatine deficiency syndromes.[6]

Mechanisms

Single-dose oral guanidinoacetic acid exhibits dose-dependent pharmacokinetics in healthy volunteers

Study Overview and Background

First study to describe the pharmacokinetic outcomes after ingestion of guanidinoacetic acid (GAA). Published in Nutrition Research in 2015 by Sergej M. Ostojic and Aleksandra Vojvodic-Ostojic from institutions in Serbia. GAA is the natural precursor of creatine in the body, synthesized endogenously from L-arginine and glycine. GAA has potential as a dietary supplement due to being more bioavailable, stable, and cost-effective than creatine. GAA is more soluble in water than creatine (3600 mg/L vs 1330 mg/L). Supplemental GAA may be "semiessential" when endogenous GAA synthesis is limited. GAA powder costs approximately 40% less than creatine.

Study Design and Methods

Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, parallel-group trial with 48 participants. 24 men and 24 women, mean age 22±2 years, weight 71±14 kg, height 176±10 cm. Participants randomly assigned to receive single oral doses of GAA (1.2g, 2.4g, 4.8g) or placebo (inulin). ⏱️ Blood samples collected before (0 hours) and at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24 hours post-ingestion. Plasma GAA and creatine assessed using high-performance liquid chromatography. One-compartment model used for pharmacokinetic analysis. ️ Standardized meals provided with no creatine-rich foods (meat, fish, milk).

Key Pharmacokinetic Findings for GAA

Lag Time and Absorption

⏰ Similar lag time across all doses: 0.14±0.17h (low), 0.31±0.18h (medium), 0.38±0.32h (high); p=0.05. Time to peak concentration: ~90 minutes for low and medium doses, 2.2 hours for high dose. ‍♂️ Mean absorption half-time: <40 minutes for low/medium doses, ~60 minutes for high dose.

Peak Concentration and Exposure

Peak plasma concentration (μmol/L): 56.9±9.2 (low), 151.3±17.5 (medium), 418.4±89.6 (high). Area under curve (AUC) increased in greater than dose-proportional manner. AUC (μmol/L*h): 224.8±38.0 (low), 531.4±64.1 (medium), 2092.4±453.2 (high); p<0.0001. Strong relationship between AUC and dose: AUC = 102.63 * e^(0.652 * Dose of GAA [g]).

Volume of distribution (L): 102.6±17.3 (low), 97.5±15.7 (medium), 61.1±12.7 (high); p<0.0001. ⏱️ Elimination half-time: similar for low/medium doses (<1.75h), longer for high dose (>2.1h); p=0.001. Apparent systemic clearance (L/h): 46.9±8.3 (low), 39.1±4.6 (medium), 20.5±4.4 (high); p<0.0001.

Ingestion of GAA elevated plasma creatine compared to placebo: +80% (1.2g), +116% (2.4g), +293% (4.8g); p<0.0001. ⏱️ Peak creatine concentration occurred within ~2 hours for all doses.
