DNSP (Dopamine Neuron Stimulating Peptides)

DNSP refers to a family of synthetic peptides derived from the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) proprotein. There are several variants including DNSP-5, DNSP-11, and others. These peptides have shown promising effects on dopaminergic neurons and potential therapeutic applications for neurodegenerative conditions like Parkinson's disease.

What is DNSP

DNSP-11 (Dopamine Neuron Stimulating Peptide-11) is a synthetic, amidated 11-amino acid neuroactive peptide derived from the human GDNF pro-domain.[2] DNSP-5 is a five amino-acid peptide that has been proposed to demonstrate biological activity relevant to neurodegenerative disease.[6] These peptides are thought to be generated from the prosequence of GDNF, which was previously believed to only assist in processing and secretion of the mature neurotrophin protein.[6] DNSP peptides are being investigated for their neurotrophic-like properties that may be relevant to the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases like Parkinson's disease.[6] The post-translational processing model of GDNF predicts that peptides like DNSP-11 and DNSP-5 are generated simultaneously and may act in concert with GDNF signaling.[6]

Benefits

DNSP-11 promotes neuronal growth and differentiation in both E14 primary neurons and dopaminergic neuron cell lines.[2] DNSP-11 provides protection against apoptosis and cell death in neuronal cells.[2] DNSP-5 significantly increases all differentiation parameters in dopaminergic neurons compared to control vehicles, suggesting neurotrophic-like properties.[6] A single intranigral infusion of DNSP-11 increases levels of dopamine (DA) and its active metabolites in the striatum of normal rats.[2] DNSP-5 exhibits neurotrophic-like effects on dopamine-producing neurons in primary cell cultures and function-enhancing effects in vivo.[7] ️ DNSP-5 shows substantial protection against staurosporine-induced cytotoxicity in neuronal cell lines.[7]

Mechanisms of Action

️ DNSP-11 activates the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 in neuronal cells, which may contribute to its neuroprotective effects.[2][5] Unlike GDNF, DNSP-11 does not directly interact with the GFRα-1 receptor, suggesting a novel mechanism of action.[5] DNSP-11 may enhance DA release by increasing DA synthesis/storage through stimulating actions on tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) activity.[5] DNSP-5 may increase the number of axonal varicosities and functional DA terminals, producing increased sites for DA synthesis and neurotransmission.[6] The effects of DNSP peptides could be particularly important in neurodegenerative diseases where neuronal plasticity phenomena are implicated.[6]

Effects on Neurotransmitters/Pathways

DNSP-11 increases potassium-evoked release of dopamine in specific subregions of the striatum two weeks after treatment.[5] A single treatment of DNSP-5 (30 μg) significantly increased (~66%) extracellular dopamine levels in the striatum compared to vehicle, while DA metabolites were unchanged.[6] DNSP-11 treatment results in phosphorylation of ERK1/2, which suggests a novel cellular mechanism responsible for increases in DA function.[5] Dopamine turnover ratios for combined metabolites were increased by DNSP-5 treatment, indicating altered dopamine metabolism.[6] The increased morphological complexity seen in vitro with DNSP-5 treatment may explain the observed increase in extracellular basal dopamine in vivo.[7]

Side Effects

️ No specific side effects of DNSP peptides were mentioned in the search results, though research appears to be in early stages.[2][4][6] It's important to note that DNSP should not be confused with DNP (2,4-dinitrophenol), which is a weight loss agent with significant acute toxicity that can lead to death.[5] (edited)

Synergies

DNSP-11 and DNSP-5 may act in concert, exhibiting synergistic effects along with GDNF signaling, as they are predicted to be generated simultaneously by the post-translational processing model.[6] The combined action of these peptides may enhance their neurotrophic effects on dopaminergic neurons.[6]

Dosage

In animal studies, a single unilateral dose (30 μg) of DNSP-5 administered to the substantia nigra showed significant effects on dopamine levels.[6] For in vitro studies, DNSP-5 was tested at several doses (0.03, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0 ng/mL) and showed significant effects on neuronal differentiation.[6] DNSP-11 was administered intranasally in animal models of Parkinson's disease, though specific dosages were not detailed in the search results.[9]

Background Information

GDNF, from which DNSP peptides are derived, has demonstrated robust effects on dopamine neuron function and survival.[5][7] Neurotrophic factors like GDNF have long been regarded as promising therapeutic agents for the treatment of Parkinson's disease.[6] A major challenge in developing disease-altering therapeutics for Parkinson's disease has been the delivery of compounds across the blood-brain barrier to the central nervous system.[9] Intranasal administration has been examined as a non-invasive delivery method for DNSP-11 to avoid issues of surgically delivering compounds to the CNS.[9] Current Parkinson's disease therapies mainly function by increasing DA levels through synthesis/metabolism modulation or receptor activation with selective agonists, but improved therapies are needed as current regimens provide only temporary symptomatic relief.[7] (edited)

Glial Cell Line-Derived Neurotrophic Factor Reverses Alcohol-Induced Allostasis of the Mesolimbic Dopaminergic System: Implications for Alcohol Reward and Seeking
We previously showed that infusion of glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) into the ventral tegmental area (VTA) rapidly reduces alcohol intake and relapse (Carnicella et al., 2008, 2009a), and increases dopamine (DA) levels in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) of alcohol-naive rats (Wang et al., 2010). Withdrawal from excessive alcohol intake is associated with a reduction in NAc DA levels, whereas drug-induced increases in NAc DA levels are associated with reward. We therefore tested whether GDNF in the VTA reverses alcohol withdrawal-associated DA deficiency and/or possesses rewarding properties. Rats were trained for 7 weeks to consume high levels of alcohol (5.47 ± 0.37 g/kg/24 h) in intermittent access to 20% alcohol in a two-bottle choice procedure. Using in vivo microdialysis, we show that 24 h withdrawal from alcohol causes a substantial reduction in NAc DA overflow, which was reversed by intra-VTA GDNF infusion. Using conditioned place preference (CPP) paradigm, we observed that GDNF on its own does not induce CPP, suggesting that the growth factor is not rewarding. However, GDNF blocked acquisition and expression of alcohol-CPP. In addition, GDNF induced a downward shift in the dose–response curve for operant self-administration of alcohol, further suggesting that GDNF suppresses, rather than substitutes for, the reinforcing effects of alcohol. Our findings suggest that GDNF reduces alcohol-drinking behaviors by reversing an alcohol-induced allostatic DA deficiency in the mesolimbic system. In addition, as it lacks abuse liability, the study further highlights GDNF as a promising target for treatment of alcohol use/abuse disorders.

Nucleus Accumbens-Derived Glial Cell Line-Derived Neurotrophic Factor Is a Retrograde Enhancer of Dopaminergic Tone in the Mesocorticolimbic System
Spontaneous firing of ventral tegmental area (VTA) dopamine (DA) neurons provides ambient levels of DA in target areas such as the nucleus accumbens (NAc) and the prefrontal cortex (PFC). Here we report that the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), produced in one target region, the NAc, is retrogradely transported by DA neurons to the VTA where the growth factor positively regulates the spontaneous firing activity of both NAc- and PFC-projecting DA neurons in a mechanism that requires the activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway.
