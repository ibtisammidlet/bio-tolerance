When injected into subcutaneous fat, MCT oil (medium-chain triglyceride oil) generally takes the body longer to metabolize compared to PEG400 (polyethylene glycol 400). Here's why: Absorption Rates: PEG400 is a water-soluble polymer that is readily absorbed into the bloodstream from subcutaneous tissue. Its hydrophilic nature allows it to diffuse quickly through the aqueous environment of the body's tissues. MCT oil is lipophilic (fat-soluble) and not as readily absorbed into the bloodstream when injected subcutaneously. Oils tend to remain in the injection site longer due to their slower diffusion through the lipid-rich environment of adipose tissue. Metabolism: PEG400 is mostly excreted unchanged by the kidneys. Because it doesn't undergo significant metabolic transformation, its clearance from the body is relatively quick once absorbed. MCT oil consists of medium-chain triglycerides that require metabolic processing. Once absorbed, MCTs are transported to the liver, where they are metabolized into energy or ketones. This metabolic process takes additional time compared to the mostly unaltered excretion of PEG400. Potential Complications: Injecting oils like MCT oil subcutaneously can lead to local inflammation, granuloma formation, or delayed absorption, all of which can prolong the presence of the oil in the tissue. PEG400 is less likely to cause such issues due to its biocompatibility and hydrophilic properties, leading to quicker absorption and elimination. Conclusion: MCT oil remains in the subcutaneous tissue longer and requires more time for absorption and metabolic processing. PEG400 is absorbed and excreted more rapidly due to its water solubility and minimal metabolic transformation. Therefore, the body takes longer to metabolize MCT oil than PEG400 when injected into subcutaneous fat.
