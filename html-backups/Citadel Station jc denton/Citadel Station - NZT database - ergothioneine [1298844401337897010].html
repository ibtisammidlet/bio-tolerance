Ergothioneine (ERG)

Ergothioneine (ERG) is a unique amino acid with powerful antioxidant properties, discovered in 1909. It's naturally produced by fungi and some bacteria but must be obtained through diet in humans, as we possess a specialized transporter (SLC22A4/OCTN1) dedicated to its uptake and distribution[9].

Brain Health

Protects against cognitive decline and memory problems associated with aging[2] ️ Shields brain cells from cortisol's harmful effects and inflammation[1] Supports mitochondrial energy production in neural cells[2] Prevents neuronal cell death caused by oxidative stress[6]

Skin Health

✨ Reduces UV-induced skin damage and photoaging[2] Protects DNA integrity in skin cells exposed to UV radiation[2] Suppresses collagen-degrading enzymes activated by UV exposure[2]

Cardiovascular Health

❤️ Best metabolic marker for reduced cardiovascular risk out of 112 markers studied[2] Associated with lower mortality risk in a 20-year follow-up study[2] Protects heart cells from oxidative stress damage[3]

Cellular Protection

️ Accumulates in tissues with high oxidative stress levels[1] Protects mitochondrial DNA from damage[4] Regenerates through interaction with glutathione[12]

Mechanism of Action

Transported via specialized SLC22A4/OCTN1 transporter throughout body[1] Acts as direct antioxidant against reactive oxygen species[2] Activates KEAP1-NRF2 pathway to upregulate antioxidant genes[4] Modulates TNF-alpha activity to reduce inflammation[3] ️ Protects mitochondrial function and reduces ROS production[4]

Quantitative Outcomes

3-30x higher antioxidant capacity than glutathione[10] Maintains ~2mM concentration in cells for oxidative stress protection[10] Levels decrease significantly with age[2]

Caveats

️ Limited human clinical trials available[2] ❗ Optimal dosage not fully established[8] Effects may vary based on individual oxidative stress levels[1] Most research is preliminary or in vitro[11]

Dosage

Recommended daily dose: 5-10mg[8] ️ Safety and efficacy not fully established for different populations[8] Natural dietary sources include mushrooms and fermented foods[10] (edited)

Ergothioneine

Introduction & Background

Ergothioneine (ET/EGT) is a naturally occurring amino acid and potent antioxidant discovered by Charles Tanret in 1909 from ergot fungus.[1,2] It's a thiourea derivative of histidine with a sulfur atom on the imidazole ring, giving exceptional stability and antioxidant capacity.[1,2] Primarily produced by fungi (mushrooms), actinomycetota, and cyanobacteria - not synthesized by animals or plants.[3] Growing interest stems from: specific transporter in mammals (OCTN1/ETT), accumulation in high-oxidative stress tissues, and epidemiological links to reduced age-related conditions.[4,5] Designated a "longevity vitamin" by biochemist Bruce Ames, suggesting importance for preventing premature aging and long-term health.[6] ️ Chemical formula C₉H₁₅N₃O₂S with molecular weight of 229.30 g/mol.[7] Features unique combination of thiol group (providing antioxidant properties) and betaine moiety (creating zwitterion at physiological pH).[7]

Biological Role & Mechanism of Action

Chemical Structure & Properties

️ Exceptional stability in physiological conditions with resistance to auto-oxidation in oxygen presence.[8] ️ Exists in tautomeric forms (thiol and thione).[8] Strong metal-chelating properties.[8] Capacity to scavenge reactive oxygen and nitrogen species.[8] Maintains antioxidant capability in both oxidizing and reducing environments, unlike glutathione and other sulfur antioxidants.[9]

Absorption, Transport & Tissue Distribution

Absorbed via OCTN1 transporter (now designated ETT - ergothioneine transporter) encoded by SLC22A4 gene.[10] Highly specific transporter with expression throughout body: small intestine, kidney proximal tubules, liver, bone marrow, erythrocytes, monocytes, and certain brain regions.[11] Highest concentrations found in: erythrocytes, bone marrow, liver, kidneys, ocular tissues, seminal fluid, and certain brain regions.[12,13] Cells lacking OCTN1/ETT don't accumulate ergothioneine as plasma membrane is virtually impermeable without the transporter.[14] ⏱️ Remarkable retention with whole-body half-life of approximately one month in humans (based on animal studies).[15]

️ Antioxidant Mechanisms

Direct scavenging of reactive oxygen species including hydroxyl radicals, hypochlorous acid, and peroxynitrite.[16] Metal chelation - binding copper and iron to prevent Fenton reactions that generate hydroxyl radicals.[17] Mitochondrial protection - concentrates in mitochondria to maintain DNA integrity by protecting against superoxide.[18] Synergistic action with glutathione and other antioxidants, creating robust defense network.[19] Activates Nrf2 pathway (master regulator of antioxidant responses), upregulating enzymes like superoxide dismutase and catalase.[20]

Anti-inflammatory Effects

Inhibits NF-κB activation, a key transcription factor regulating pro-inflammatory cytokine expression.[21] Decreases production of interleukin-8, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and other inflammatory mediators.[22] Protects cellular membranes during oxidative stress, preventing inflammatory mediator release.[23]

Mitochondrial Function Enhancement

Improves mitochondrial respiration and oxygen utilization efficiency.[24] ️ Protects mitochondrial DNA, which is vulnerable to oxidative damage due to proximity to ROS production.[25] ️ Maintains mitochondrial membrane potential, supporting efficient energy production.[26]

Dietary Sources & Supplementation

Natural Food Sources

Richest sources are mushrooms, with varying concentrations (dry weight):[27,28] Oyster mushrooms: 119.0 mg/kg King oyster mushrooms: 74.0 mg/kg Shiitake mushrooms: 27.0 mg/kg Maitake mushrooms: 20.4 mg/kg Button mushrooms: 7.3 mg/kg Other sources in smaller amounts:[27,28] Black turtle beans: 13.49 mg/kg Red kidney beans: 4.52 mg/kg Oat bran: 4.41 mg/kg Liver (chicken): 10.78 mg/kg Fermented foods like tempeh and miso ️ Daily intake estimates range from 0.1 to 7 mg, higher in populations regularly consuming mushrooms.[29] Cooking doesn't significantly reduce ergothioneine content as it remains stable under most cooking conditions.[30]

Bioavailability

⏱️ Readily absorbed from mushroom sources with plasma levels peaking within 1-2 hours of consumption.[31] Efficiently taken up via specific OCTN1/ETT transporter from intestine to tissues expressing the transporter.[32] Minimal metabolism with estimated one-month whole-body half-life.[33] Tissue levels can be significantly increased through dietary supplementation.[34]

Supplementation Forms

Synthetic ergothioneine - chemically identical to natural compound but historically expensive to produce. Fungal extracts - standardized extracts from mushrooms rich in ergothioneine. Fermentation-derived ergothioneine - produced through precision fermentation, more economically viable.[35] Commercially available in capsule or powder form, with dosages ranging from 5-30 mg per serving.

Dosage Considerations

✅ US FDA reviewed safety at doses up to 30 mg/day for supplements and 5 mg for addition to food products (GRAS Notice 734).[36] Recent human trials investigated dosages of 5-30 mg daily with positive effects observed.[37,38] Traditional mushroom-rich diets provide 7-10 mg daily, suggesting this as potentially beneficial target.[39] ⏳ Long half-life suggests benefits may accumulate over time with consistent supplementation.

❤️ Health Benefits

Cardiovascular Health

Large Swedish cohort study (21.4-year follow-up) found higher plasma ergothioneine associated with significantly lower risk of coronary disease (HR=0.85), cardiovascular mortality (HR=0.79), and overall mortality (HR=0.86).[40] Protects vascular endothelial cells from oxidative damage, critical in atherosclerosis prevention.[41] ️ Inhibits LDL cholesterol oxidation, preventing key initiating event in atherosclerosis.[42] Animal studies show protection against ischemia-reperfusion injury in heart and liver tissue.[43]

Cognitive Function & Neurological Health

Lower plasma ergothioneine associated with cognitive decline in elderly; levels decrease with age and cognitive impairment.[44] Research shows lower levels in patients with neurodegenerative diseases.[45] Recent double-blind, placebo-controlled trial showed ergothioneine supplementation supported cognitive function, memory, and sleep in elderly with subjective memory complaints.[46] ️ Neuroprotective mechanisms include:[47] Mitigating oxidative stress in neural tissues Protecting mitochondrial function in neurons Reducing neuroinflammation Enhancing BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) levels Increasing expression of PSD95 (protein crucial for synaptic plasticity) Can cross blood-brain barrier via OCTN1/ETT transporter expressed in cerebral vasculature.[48]

Exercise Performance & Recovery

Mouse study showed 41% increase in running time before exhaustion after one week of supplementation.[49] Boosts mitochondrial respiration and improves oxygen utilization during exercise.[50] Decreases markers of oxidative damage following intense exercise.[51] Lowers inflammatory biomarkers after strenuous activity.[52] Improves performance without interfering with early muscle recovery signaling pathways essential for training adaptations.[53]

Metabolic Health

Higher plasma levels associated with lower risk of metabolic syndrome components.[54] Preliminary research indicates potential improvement in insulin sensitivity and glucose handling.[55] ️ May mitigate tissue damage from hyperglycemia, potentially reducing diabetic complication risk.[56] Studies suggest protection of liver cells from oxidative damage and inflammation, key in NAFLD prevention.[57]

Skin Health & Photoprotection

️ Protects skin cells from UV-induced damage through antioxidant properties.[58] May help preserve collagen integrity and slow skin aging processes.[59] Open-label clinical study showed daily oral supplementation improved skin health parameters including moisture retention, elasticity, and texture.[60] ️ May protect skin from environmental pollutants and external stressors accelerating skin aging.[61]

️ Other Potential Benefits

️ Accumulates in ocular tissues, potentially protecting against conditions like cataracts and age-related macular degeneration.[62] May help preserve kidney function through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.[63] Potential benefits for respiratory health by inhibiting NF-κB activation and reducing inflammatory cytokines in alveolar epithelial cells.[64] High concentration in seminal fluid suggests role in protecting sperm from oxidative damage.[65] Research indicates potential for reducing oxidative stress in placenta, supporting healthy pregnancy outcomes.[66]

Dosage, Safety & Side Effects

️ Safety Profile

✅ FDA GRAS status for adults at doses up to 30 mg/day for supplements and 5 mg for food products (GRN #734).[67] Toxicological evaluations found no evidence of genotoxicity, mutagenicity, or reproductive toxicity at therapeutic doses.[68] Long history of consumption in mushrooms with no identified adverse effects. Human clinical trials report no significant adverse events related to supplementation.[69,70]

Recommended Dosages

General health maintenance: 5-10 mg daily (similar to mushroom-rich diets). Specific health goals: 10-30 mg daily (consistent with clinical trial dosages). Upper limit: 30 mg daily (highest dose reviewed for safety by regulators). ️ Optimal dosages for specific conditions not yet firmly established; individual needs may vary.

️ Potential Interactions and Contraindications

Limited evidence suggests potential interactions with gabapentin, though clinical significance unclear.[71] Theoretical caution warranted in autoimmune disorders until more research available. Limited safety data in pregnancy and lactation suggests cautious approach. Individuals with SLC22A4 gene variants may have altered ergothioneine transport and potentially different supplementation responses.[72]

Clinical Evidence & Case Studies

Clinical Trials

Cognitive function trial (Blue California, 2024): Double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial showed ErgoActive® ergothioneine supported cognitive function, memory, and sleep in elderly with subjective memory complaints over four months.[73] ErgMS pilot study: Randomized controlled trial examining effects in people with metabolic syndrome.[74] Skin health study: Open-label clinical study found daily oral supplementation improved skin health parameters (moisture retention, elasticity, texture).[75] Pharmacokinetics studies confirmed ready absorption from dietary sources with significant tissue retention.[76]

Epidemiological Evidence

❤️ Swedish cohort study (Smith et al., 2020): Large prospective study with 21.4-year follow-up linked higher plasma ergothioneine with reduced risk of coronary disease, cardiovascular mortality, and overall mortality.[77] Research by Cheah et al. showed ergothioneine levels decrease with age and are lower in those experiencing cognitive decline.[78] Studies found lower ergothioneine levels in patients with neurodegenerative diseases versus healthy controls.[79]

Mechanistic Studies

Laboratory studies confirmed ability to neutralize various reactive oxygen/nitrogen species while preserving cellular redox balance.[80] Research showed inhibition of NF-κB activation and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine production in various cell types.[81] Studies documented improved mitochondrial respiration and protection of mitochondrial DNA from oxidative damage.[82] Neuronal cell model research demonstrated protection against neurotoxic insults and promotion of neural health/plasticity factors.[83]

Conclusion

Ergothioneine represents a promising compound at the intersection of nutrition, aging, and disease prevention. Its unique antioxidant properties appear important for protecting tissues from oxidative stress, supporting mitochondrial function, and reducing inflammation—key factors in aging and age-related diseases. Designation as "longevity vitamin" by Bruce Ames aligns with evidence suggesting importance for long-term health. Specific OCTN1/ETT transporter facilitating absorption/retention in high-oxidative stress tissues supports biological significance. ❤️ Current evidence suggests benefits for cardiovascular health, cognitive function, exercise performance, metabolic health, and skin health. ✅ Excellent safety profile makes ergothioneine intriguing for supplementation, especially as modern diets may provide suboptimal amounts.

Future Research Questions

❓ What are optimal dosages for specific health outcomes? How do SLC22A4 gene variations affect individual responses? ⏱️ Can long-term supplementation effectively slow biological aging aspects? How does ergothioneine interact with other dietary components and lifestyle factors?
